# The Call System — 3 Calls

The machine is three calls. Each has ONE job:

| Call | When | Job | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Day 0 (booked via /apply) | Qualify + set up the free design | Intake list sent, reveal call booked |
| 2. Design Reveal | ~Day 5 | THE CLOSE — the design sells, you just present | Approval + 50% + kickoff booked |
| 3. Kickoff | Right after approval | Start the build clean | Launch date locked, build starts |

You never sell on Call 1. The design closes on Call 2. Call 3 is logistics.

---

## CALL 1 — Discovery (30 min, Day 0)

### Before (10 min, mandatory)

- [ ] Open their store on your PHONE. Write down 2–3 leaks (buried reviews, slow load, no express checkout, weak product page).
- [ ] Check /apply answers: option picked, revenue band.
- [ ] Meta Ad Library: are they running ads? What does the ad promise? (Their ad = your design's message-match input.)

### The 30 minutes

**1. Frame (2 min)** — say almost verbatim:

> "Here's how I run this — I'll ask about your numbers, tell you what I saw in your store, and walk you through how the free design works. If you're a fit, I start designing this week and you see it in about five days. If you're not, I'll tell you straight. Sound good?"

**2. Their numbers (8 min)** — ask in order, write everything down (this feeds the design AND the baseline):

1. "Where's your traffic from, what are you spending a month?"
2. "What's your conversion rate right now?"
3. "Average order value?"
4. "Sessions a month, roughly?"
5. "What have you tried already?"
6. The money question: **"If the site converted half a percent higher, what's that worth to you per month?"** — let THEM say the number. You'll repeat their number back to them on Call 2.

**Baseline ritual:** have them share their screen and screenshot the 30-day conversion rate, sessions, AOV together, on this call. Say why: "This is the number I have to beat. I want us both looking at the same one." (This is the guarantee's foundation — never skip it.) While they're sharing, also glance at device split and top products — that's all the analytics the design needs, so you never have to ask for account access before they've paid.

**3. Diagnose (5 min)** — the proof-of-competence moment:

> "I went through your store before this. Two things jumped out…"

Specific ("reviews are below the fold on mobile") beats general ("could be optimized"). Two real observations, max three.

**4. The deal (5 min)** — walk the timeline verbatim, same as the site:

> "Day zero is today. By about day five you'll have your new homepage design — free, you pay nothing to that point. We get on a call, I show it to you next to your current site. If you love it, that's when the fifty percent happens — $2,250 — and the build starts. Live by day fourteen or you don't pay the rest. After launch, unlimited revisions and I don't stop until it beats the number we just screenshotted. Total is $4,500 — half at design approval, half at launch."

**Price rule:** say the price in the same flat tone as the dates. Then stop talking. First person to speak after the price loses.

**5. Fit decision (5 min)** — run the checklist:

**Fit (all four):** running real traffic now · they're the decision maker · brand assets exist · under ~100 SKUs

**Not fit (any one):** no traffic · dropshipping · 3+ approvers · needs a logo first · needs ERP/custom app work

Not a fit? Say it straight:

> "Honestly — you're not a fit for this yet, and I don't want to design something free that can't pay off for you. Here's what I'd do first: [one genuine pointer]. When you're there, come back."

Disqualifying out loud is what makes "3 builds a month" true.

**6. Close Call 1 (5 min)** — the close here is NOT the sale. It's two bookings:

> "Alright, you're a fit — I'll take it. Two things: I'm sending you the intake list right after this — just your logo and product photos, that's it. Get me those within 48 hours so your five days start now. And let's book the reveal call while we're here — that's where you see your design. What's [day ~5] look like?"

Book the reveal call ON this call. "I'll book later" = 50% show rate.

## CALL 2 — Design Reveal (20–30 min, ~Day 5) — THE CLOSE

The design does the selling. Your job is to present it so the decision is obvious.

### Before

- [ ] Design done, side-by-side comparison ready (their current homepage next to yours — same products, same screen)
- [ ] Their Call-1 numbers in front of you, especially THEIR money answer
- [ ] 50% invoice drafted, ready to send the minute they approve

### Structure

**1. Re-anchor (2 min):**

> "Quick recap — you're at [X]% conversion, [their AOV], and you told me half a percent more is worth about [THEIR number] a month. Keep that number in your head while you look at this."

**2. The reveal (10 min).** Show current homepage first, then yours. Walk it top to bottom, and tie every choice to something THEY said on Call 1:

> "You told me most of your traffic is from [their ad] — so the first thing they see now matches it. You said people ask about [objection] — that's answered right here, above the fold…"

Never say "I think it looks better." Say what each piece does to the number.

**3. Ask for the decision (say it exactly):**

> "So — straight question. Is this an upgrade on what you have?"

(They say yes — it will be, that's why you filtered on Call 1.)

> "Then here's the next step: fifty percent — $2,250 — and this goes from a picture to your live store by day fourteen. I'll send the invoice now and we book your kickoff. Good?"

Then silence. Again.

**4. If they waver:** the objections live here, not Call 1 —

- **"I need to think about it."** → "Sure. The design's yours to look at for seven days — after that the slot goes to the next store. But tell me now: is there something in the design you don't love? Because that I can fix today."
- **"Can I show my partner/team?"** → (You filtered for decision-maker — so:) "Of course. What's YOUR read though — if it were only your call, is it a yes?" Isolate the real objection from the excuse.
- **"Why 50% now?"** → "You've seen exactly what you're buying — that's the point of doing the design first. The build starts the day you pay, and it's live in nine more days."
- **"Can we change X?"** → "Yes — one consolidated revision round is built in. Approving the direction is what starts the clock; the tweaks happen inside it."

**5. Either way, end clean:**
- **Approved** → invoice sent on the call, kickoff booked on the call (within 1–2 days).
- **Not yet** → "Seven days. Day two I'll check in, day seven the slot releases." Then actually do that: one nudge day 2, one final day 5–6, release day 7. Never chase past the window — the window is the offer.

## CALL 3 — Kickoff (15 min, right after approval)

Pure logistics. The sale is done — don't reopen it.

- [ ] Payment confirmed before the call starts
- [ ] **Store access set up NOW** (collaborator invite + analytics viewer) — this is the right moment to ask: they've paid, the build needs it
- [ ] Final asset check: everything from intake received and usable?
- [ ] Re-confirm the baseline numbers (same screenshots from Call 1 — "this is still the number to beat")
- [ ] Walk the build days: "Build through day [X]. One consolidated feedback round on [day]. QA and a live test purchase on [day]. Launch on [date]." Name real dates, not day numbers.
- [ ] Set the communication rule: "You'll hear from me at every milestone. Feedback goes in one batch — that's how it stays on 14 days."
- [ ] Clock rule, said kindly: "Days waiting on your feedback pause the clock — that keeps the guarantee honest for both of us."

Ends with: launch date said out loud, in writing after the call.

---

## After every call, same day

- Call 1 done → intake email within the hour (see FREE-DESIGN-ONBOARDING.md)
- Any call ends with a next step → that next step is booked before hanging up, never "I'll send a link"
- Check which ad brought them (UTM in the lead email). After 5 discovery calls you'll know which ad sends closers, not clickers.

## No-shows (any call)

Calendly reminders at 24h + 1h do the prevention. If they no-show: one message, one reschedule, once:

> "Missed you today — want the slot rebooked? Fair warning, this month's builds are going."

No second reschedule. Your calendar is the product.
